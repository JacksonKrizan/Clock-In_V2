Career Deep Dive · 03 · The Jobs

Firefighter

Operate gear and respond to emergencies to save lives — our next career, with the level built and the systems ready.

● IN PROGRESS Scene: Firemap.unity Art: FirefightersPack Teaches: emergency response
Where it stands today

Firefighter is the career we're actively building. The Firemap.unity scene already exists (a substantial ~16 MB level), a dedicated FirePlayerController prefab is wired into the multiplayer spawn system, and firefighting art assets are in the project. What's left is the gameplay scripting — so this page covers the groundwork that's done, the design we're building toward, and the plan to finish it.

How it was made groundwork laid

The foundation is real and in-repo; the mechanic scripts are the remaining piece.

What already exists

  • Firemap.unity — the environment is built and laid out as a full scene.
  • FirePlayerController.prefab — a per-career player prefab, alongside GamingPlayerController and AutoPlayerController, so the networked spawn system already knows how to drop a firefighter into this scene.
  • FirefightersPack assets — firefighting art/props are imported and available.
  • Shared systems — the same ScoreManager / ScoreNetworkSync and Photon room flow the other jobs use are ready to plug into.

What still needs building

  • A Assets/_Scripts/Firemap/ folder — today there's no script implementation for the job.
  • A hose-control mechanic: raycast to aim, hold to spray (it can reuse the proven first-person input model from the other jobs).
  • Fire objects that extinguish under sustained spray, and fire-spread logic to nearby flammable props over time.
  • A rescue/objective scenario plus scoring wired into ScoreManager.

How it's played intended design

The target gameplay loop we're designing toward.

Respond to the call

You spawn into the Firemap scene as the firefighter — an active emergency is underway.

Gear up & aim

Operate firefighting gear; aim the hose with a forward raycast, the same interaction backbone the other jobs already use.

Fight the fire

Hold to spray; sustained contact extinguishes a fire. Move fast — fire spreads to nearby flammable objects if you're too slow.

Save lives

Reach and rescue civilians within the time limit; minimize collateral damage.

Scored & synced

Speed and efficiency feed the shared scoreboard, synced across the room over Photon like every other job.

What it will teach

  • Emergency response — staying calm and acting fast under pressure.
  • Prioritization & triage — deciding what to tackle first when everything's urgent.
  • Cause & effect — fire spreads if ignored; early action contains it.

How to finish & improve it

A concrete build path from the current scene to a complete, polished job.

Phase 1 — make it playable

  • Create Assets/_Scripts/Firemap/ with a HoseController (aim + spray) reusing the shared first-person input.
  • A Fire component with a health value that drops while sprayed and extinguishes at zero.
  • A FireManager to track active fires, end the round, and report the score.
  • Wire scoring into the existing ScoreManager so it shows on the live scoreboard immediately.

Phase 2 — depth

  • Fire-spread logic: untended fire jumps to nearby flammable props over time, creating urgency.
  • Civilian rescue objectives with a countdown and a "saved / lost" tally.
  • Difficulty scaling: more ignition points and faster spread as the score climbs.
  • Particle + audio feedback (flame, steam on extinguish, alarms) — leaning on the FirefightersPack assets.

Phase 3 — multiplayer & polish

  • Network fire state over Photon (master-client authoritative) so a fire extinguished by one player reads out for everyone — learning from the networking game's duplicate-scoring pitfall.
  • Co-op roles: one player on the hose, another guiding rescues.
  • A pre-mission briefing and post-mission review (fires out, civilians saved, time, damage).

Educational value

  • In-the-moment tips on real fire science — the fire triangle (heat, fuel, oxygen), why you cut off one side to stop a fire.
  • Stop-drop-roll and evacuation-order callouts framed as gameplay guidance.
  • Tie the score to real response metrics so the lesson sticks.
