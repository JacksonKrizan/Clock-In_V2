Auto Mechanic

Grab, rotate and fit the right parts to repair and rebuild a car — a hands-on physics assembly job.

Scene: AutoShop.unity Physics-based assembly Single-player (today) Teaches: assembly & precision work

How it was made

Built around a physics grab system plus a specialized wheel-socket mechanic, in Assets/_Scripts/AutoMap/ and its Arc/ subfolder.

The core scripts

  • PickUp.cs — the main grab/carry/throw controller. Press E near a part (forward raycast, tag-matched against a carParts list) to pick it up; it becomes kinematic and parents to holdPos. OnCollisionEnter fires a Unity UnityEvent<Collision>, so designers can wire reactions in the Inspector.
  • WheelAttach.cs (Arc/) — the wheel-specific mechanic. OnTriggerStay detects the player nearby; press E to grab the wheel, F to attach. AttachWheel() snaps the wheel to identity local position/rotation, disables its collider, and makes it kinematic.
  • Parts.cs (Arc/) — an alternative trigger-based picking system, with separate Parts and PickUpWheel classes.
  • TestVarList.cs / RecivingList.cs — scaffolding: a simple partList container and a loop that logs over it.
  • CompareTags.cs — an empty stub (planned tag-matching that wasn't implemented).

Unity techniques in play

  • Forward-raycast pickup with kinematic state toggling and a dedicated hold layer — the same proven carry model as the networking game.
  • Trigger-volume proximity — wheels use OnTriggerStay against the Player tag rather than a raycast, so you attach by standing close.
  • Snap-to-socket attachment — attaching zeroes the wheel's local transform so it clicks cleanly into the car model.
  • Inspector-wired collisions — UnityEvent hooks make part impacts designer-configurable without code.
Honest engineering notes

PickUp.cs is ~99% identical to the networking game's IPdraggable.cs — the carry logic was copied rather than shared. There's no Photon integration here yet, so the job is single-player only. There's also no scoring call and no round/timer, and AttachWheel2() is half-finished. Several files (CompareTags, TestVarList, RecivingList) are stubs or debug scaffolding.

How it's played

Find the part, line it up, lock it in.

Enter the shop

You spawn in an auto shop with parts (wheels and components) scattered around a car that needs repair.

Pick up a part

Press E near a part to grab it. It becomes kinematic and rides in your hands at holdPos.

Line it up

Hold R + move the mouse to rotate the part so it faces the right way for its socket.

Attach it

For a wheel, press F near the socket (tagged Tirespot) — it snaps into place, collision off, locked in. Or left-click to throw a part away.

Finish the build

The goal is to fit every required part to its correct spot and rebuild the car.

What it teaches

  • Assembly — parts go together in a specific, deliberate way.
  • Precision & orientation — a part only fits when it's aligned correctly.
  • Why maintenance matters — the README framing ties each task to a real reason (correct tire PSI, healthy engine oil).

How it could be improved

This job has the most runway — here's where it pays off.

Bugs & correctness

  • No placement validation — any part can "attach" wherever it collides. Add socket-type checks so a wheel only fits a tire socket.
  • Duplicated carry code — PickUp.cs clones IPdraggable.cs; every fix must be made twice. Extract one shared PhysicsGrab component.
  • Silent attach failures — missing Collider/Rigidbody on a wheel prefab fails quietly; log warnings.
  • Dead code — remove the empty CompareTags, the debug-spam RecivingList, and finish or delete AttachWheel2().

Gameplay depth

  • An assembly sequence / checklist ("wheels first, then check oil") instead of everything available at once.
  • Correctness feedback — a sound and "Wheel Attached!" message on a good fit.
  • Part metadata (name, socket type, weight, size) so placement can be validated and graded.
  • Damage states and a timer ("finish before the customer arrives") for urgency.

Educational value

  • Tooltips explaining the why: "Wheels distribute weight — check PSI," "Old oil loses viscosity," "A clogged filter cuts fuel efficiency."
  • A diagnosis → sourcing → installation flow, instead of installation alone ("Engine runs rough → likely a bad spark plug").
  • A safety callout to model real shop practice.

Multiplayer & architecture

  • Add PhotonView + IPunObservable so two players can repair one car together (one holds, one guides).
  • Part grab-locks / ownership to resolve two players reaching for the same wheel.
  • Hook into the shared ScoreManager so repairs count on the live scoreboard like deliveries do.
  • Consolidate scattered inputs (E/F/R/click) into one input manager, and model carry state as a small state machine.
